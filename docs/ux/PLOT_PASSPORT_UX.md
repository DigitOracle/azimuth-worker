# Plot Passport: UX research note and design decisions

Najma, More, then PLOT PASSPORT. Built 2 Nov 2026 on branch `plot-passport`. Not deployed.

**What we are delivering:** one plot, every connected record, every claim traceable. A broker or a client can say a plot number out loud, and inside three taps see what the plot is, what is on it or planned for it, and how sure each link is. A researcher can take away a dated report that cites its sources.

## 1. Research (short)

| Source | What we took from it |
|---|---|
| HM Land Registry, "Search for land and property information" ([GOV.UK](https://gov.uk/get-information-about-property-and-land/search-the-register), [title register explained](https://hmlandregistry.blog.gov.uk/2018/05/10/the-abc-of-title-registers/)) | You can search by several keys (postcode, address, map, title number) and get a short free summary first, with the full register one step further. We copy that: one search box that accepts any key, a summary card first, the detail one tap away. The register is split into fixed sections (property, owner, charges), which is why our passport has fixed, always-present sections. |
| Regrid parcel data and app ([schema](https://support.regrid.com/articles/schema), [2025 residential fields](https://regrid.com/blog/2025-schema-residential-property-data)) | A parcel card leads with identity (number, address, area, land use) and then groups the rest. Exports are a report rather than raw data, which suits our research-only rule. |
| NN/g on progressive disclosure ([video](https://www.nngroup.com/videos/progressive-disclosure/); summary at [The Decision Lab](https://thedecisionlab.com/reference-guide/design/progressive-disclosure)) | Show the few things most people need first and put the rest one step away. Do not hide what changes a decision. So we show the summary sentence, the research flags and the strength chips up front; how each link was made sits behind "How it was linked". |
| Uncertainty for non-experts: Padilla, Kay and Hullman, *Uncertainty Visualization* ([PDF](https://moodle2.units.it/pluginfile.php/466375/mod_resource/content/0/Uncertainty_Visualization_Padilla_Kay_Hullman_2020.pdf)); van der Bles et al. and the Frontiers review ([article](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/computer-science/articles/10.3389/fcomp.2020.590232/full)); [Bhatt et al., Uncertainty as transparency](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2011.07586) | Plain-word categories are easy to read, but people do not know where the lines between them fall. So every category label needs its rule in one sentence. People also tend to read an estimate as a fact unless told plainly. Our answer is three named strengths, each with its rule one tap away and a legend, and a separate view for the weaker links. The numeric score is kept for the detail and the report. |

Dubai REST (the Dubai Land Department app) and DLD property status pages were not opened in this session: they need a UAE Pass sign-in, which we do not enter. Their published pattern is a status card per unit or project, which matches our summary card.

## 2. Design decisions

1. **Three taps.** (1) Search with live suggestions. It accepts a parcel number (3190268), a municipality plot number (319-268), an area and land number (388/80), a Najma id, or a project or building name. Pressing Enter opens the top match. (2) The passport. (3) The research report (print or save as PDF), or "How it was linked" on any source. A link straight to a plot (`?id=`) opens it in one tap, so a passport can be shared in a message.
1a. **Browse by place, for people who do not know the number** (Kendall, 2 Nov: "users won't know parcel numbers"). Above the search box: **1 District**, then **2 Building or project**, then the plot.
   - *Why:* NN/g's work on browse and search ([Search vs. navigate](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/search-visible-and-simple/), [the scent of information](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/information-scent/)) shows that people who cannot name what they want rely on a hierarchy whose labels they recognise. A district and a tower name are what a broker or client actually knows. HM Land Registry offers a map and address route beside the title-number search for the same reason.
   - *Pickers:* each step opens a sheet. It rises from the bottom on a phone and sits centred on desktop. Every sheet has a type-ahead filter, because Business Bay alone lists about 170 buildings and projects. The filter matches the first letters of any word (NN/g, [Mobile dropdowns](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/drop-down-menus/): long native dropdowns are hard to use on a phone; a filterable full-width list is the alternative).
   - *Accessibility:* the sheet has `role=dialog` with a label and focus goes into the filter. Enter picks the top match, Escape closes the sheet, and focus returns to the step button.
   - *Vacant land:* "Plots without a building" is always offered first in a district and stays visible while filtering. Plots with buildings but no registered name are grouped under "Buildings with no registered name", so no plot is unreachable.
   - *Three taps:* a building on one plot opens its passport straight away (tap 1 district, tap 2 building). A project or master community on several plots lists them (tap 3). Picking a district opens its building list without an extra tap.
   - *Breadcrumb:* District > Building > Plot sits above the summary. The district crumb reopens that district's buildings, and the building crumb reopens its plots. A plot opened from search shows its own district.
   - *Data:* `pp_dl` lists the districts. `pp_d_<district>` holds `{name, groups: [[name, kind, [[plot id, label]]]], vacant}`. Groups are capped at 400 plots and vacant land at 1,500 per district. Business Bay comes to about 60 KB.
2. **Plain words first.** The summary card is a single sentence a non-specialist can read out: area in sq m and sq ft, land use, district, what is on the plot, what is under way, and the date of the last recorded change.
3. **One fixed order of cards:** Research flags, Land, Projects, Permits, Buildings, Timeline, Sources. A strip of section links jumps to any of them. Each card shows three items, then "Show all". Every card is present even when it is empty, and says so in words ("No building permit is linked to this plot"), because an absence is information too.
4. **Strength chips on every fact.** *Direct match* (solid dot): the record carries this plot's own number. *Map-based estimate* (hollow dot): the link comes from a location or from a number quoted in text. *Project-level only* (square): the record belongs to the project, not the plot. Tapping a chip shows its one-line rule. Colour is never the only signal, because each chip also has a dot shape and a word.
5. **A confidence view.** The default view is **Direct facts**. **Everything we hold** adds the map-based sources and a separate dashed card, *Inferred associations*, for project-level links. That card says that these describe the project, not the plot, and are never added up per plot. In the default view a note says how many more sources exist, so nothing is silently hidden.
6. **Research flags are neutral,** in blue rather than red or amber, and are worded as gaps or disagreements, never as a judgement on the plot. Examples: no direct links, permits but no registered building, the land register and the buildings register naming the project differently, no plot area, not placed in a district, some links by location only.
7. **Sources are traceable.** Each card gives the publisher in words (for example "Building permits (Dubai Municipality)"), how the record was linked, in words, and the number of keys. "How it was linked" adds the table name, the link strength out of 100, how much of that source reaches any plot, and its as-of date. Our own crosswalk tables are not listed as evidence.
8. **The report** is on a light background, prints on A4, and turns its tables into stacked rows at phone width. It includes the label key, flags, land, projects, permits, buildings, timeline and every source with its method and date. Footer: *Prepared for Dr. Digital Abbot by Najma. Research use only: not a legal title, survey or valuation.*
9. **House rules.** Cards, not lists, at 375 px with no sideways scroll. Every tap target is at least 44 px. Light and dark follow Najma's `naj_thm` setting or the device. Line icons only, never emoji. Abbreviations are spelled out (Dubai Land Department, Dubai Municipality, sq m and sq ft). The text says "building outline", never "Blocks" or "digital twin".
10. **What is left out on purpose.** No DEWA data of any kind: an account is not a person or a household. No Google place content, because the terms bar caching names and ratings and bar showing them off a Google map. No Abu Dhabi tables. No rents or sales "on this plot": those exist only at project level. No map on this page.

## 3. Data path

`naj-market-pulse/scripts/build_plot_passport.py` reads MotherDuck `najma`: `lk_plot_master`, `lk_plot_xwalk`, `najma_plot_coverage`, DLD buildings, DM permits and DM projects. It writes these KV keys: `pp_meta`, `pp_s_<two characters>` (search shards) and `pp_b_<crc32(id) % 4096>` (passports, about 65 per key), plus `wrangler kv bulk put` batch files. It never calls wrangler. When the Trust workstream publishes `v_plot_passport` and `v_plot_evidence` (contract in `docs/PLOT_INTEL_VIEWS.md`), swap the two SELECTs marked VIEW-SWAP. The page shape does not change.

The worker reads those keys only, in `src/plot_passport.js`. It uses the same key check as the other Najma rooms (`clientOk`), and owner links are removed on client keys (`clientResp`).

## 4. Simplified, 2 Nov 2026 (supersedes decisions 3, 4, 5, 7 and the report contents in 8)

Dr. Kendall Wilson: "simplify plot passport, too much information. We need: timeline; are there any flats available, if so who are the agents; the buildings tab; we do not need the sources." Then: add prices.

- **Layout:** search and District > Building > Plot browse stay; then the summary card (one plain sentence, report and copy-link buttons, and at most one small line "N research flags on this plot — see Data checks" linking to `/data_checks`); then four tabs, one tap each: **Flats for sale/rent | Prices | Buildings | Timeline**. Default tab: Flats when any current advert exists, else Buildings.
- **Removed from the page:** the Sources card, "How it was linked", the "Direct facts / Everything we hold" toggle, the Inferred associations card, the Land / Projects / Permits cards and the label legend. Strength survives only as a small dot per fact (solid = direct match, hollow = map-based estimate) with a one-line key under the tabs.
- **Flats:** current adverts only (in the building's newest capture, captured within 7 days). Grouped by building, a count line ("372 flats advertised: 372 to rent"), cards with beds, size, price (sale or annual rent), listed date, portal, and the agent: name, brokerage, the agent's own phone as a call button (never Property Finder's shared WhatsApp line), and the Land Department broker card where `lk_agent_broker_link` holds it. None: "No flats advertised on this plot right now (checked <date>)".
- **Prices:** two grouped bar charts drawn as inline SVG: median annual rent (new Ejari contracts) and median purchase price (registered sales), 2024, 2025, 2026 to date, 1/2/3 bed, value labels in AED k / m, deal count under each bar. Labelled "This project" or "This building", never a plot fact. Own deals are used whenever there is at least one; only a year/bed with zero own deals falls back to the district median, drawn lighter and marked "d" with its count. Definitions are those of `scripts/build_bedroom_medians.py`.
- **Buildings:** compact cards: name, floors, units, completion, status. **Timeline:** dated one-liners, newest first.
- **Report:** matches the page: flats with agents, the two price tables, buildings, timeline. No sources appendix.
- **Data:** each passport gains `flats` (cap 50 adverts, newest first, with totals) and `prices`, from `naj-market-pulse/scripts/plot_passport_market.py`; listings come from the local lake (the `lst_*` tables are not in MotherDuck), prices from `data/chat/chat.duckdb`.
